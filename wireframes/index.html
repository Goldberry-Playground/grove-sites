Gather at the Grove —
four sister sites,
sixteen wireframes.

Static HTML+CSS design studies for the village: the hub (gatheringatthegrove.com) plus the three retail-facing properties — Goldberry Grove Farm, At The Grove Nursery, and GGG Woodworking. The hub is the parent in the IA — blog-forward, community-rooted — and links into the three shops with each brand's accent color signalling the handoff. Each site commits to a distinct aesthetic category so they never read as recolored templates.

One village, four sites,
four typographic worlds.

Four sister brands with overlapping audiences are at constant risk of looking like the same template recolored. The protection is to commit to different aesthetic categories — editorial-contemplative (hub), editorial-heritage (Goldberry), documentary-field-guide (nursery), brutalist-catalog (GGG) — not just different palettes.

A visitor moving between any two of these sites reads them as siblings (shared design seriousness, shared lineage in the footer, the persistent sibling-strip nav at the top) rather than as recolored siblings. That's the goal: family resemblance without uniformity.

The cross-site cohesion bar — the thin black sibling-strip at the very top of the hub site, listing all four properties — is the anti-jarring move. The same strip will retrofit onto the three storefronts so the navigation frame is identical across all four sites even though the content beneath is dramatically different. (Storefront retrofit is a follow-up edit to the 12 storefront HTML files.)

The font pairs were chosen so no typeface appears in two of the four sites. The palettes share a parchment-family neutral but otherwise diverge in saturation and temperature. The grid logic is fundamentally different on each — cinematic hero, magazine asymmetry, documentary panorama, catalog grid. A user could not mistake one site's home page for another's.
