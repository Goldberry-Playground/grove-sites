The catalog you're reading was
built by digging nine hundred trees a year.

At The Grove was started in 2018 by Josh and Abigail Dunbar on seventy acres of upland hardpan in the Appalachian foothills of West Virginia. The first season we sold forty-six trees. The second season we sold one hundred. We've grown roughly thirty percent every year since, which sounds like a lot until you remember it's compounding against a base of forty-six.

We propagate everything in-house: scion wood collected in January, grafted onto rootstock in March, lined out in the propagation beds in April, and lifted bare-root the following February at two years old. Nothing in our catalog has been bought-in from a wholesale supplier. We can tell you which row, in which bed, every tree in this season's catalog was lifted from.

That matters because cold-climate propagation is not a corner you can cut. A tree that was started in Oregon and shipped East to a nursery to be "finished" looks identical in the catalog and behaves very differently in your soil. We don't ship anything we didn't grow.

A timeline of the propagation beds.

2004

Eli & Rosa buy the seventy acres.

The land was a failed dairy. Hardpan, three feet of topsoil in the bottom and six inches up top. The first job was a soil-amendment program nobody recommended and almost everyone said wouldn't work. It worked.

2006

First grafted trees go to market.

Sixty-eight trees, eleven varieties. Sold out of the back of a pickup truck at the regional farmer's market on a Saturday in April.

2009

The first cold-storage barn.

Built from reclaimed timbers off the old dairy. Holds 2,000 bare-root trees at 34°F using nothing but insulation, a single fan, and the New England winter.

2022

The catalog goes to a hundred varieties.

The mail-order business eclipses the in-person sales for the first time. We hire two more staff and add a second propagation bed.

2018

The Field Guide goes online.

Twelve years of variety profiles, planting guides, and propagation notes get rewritten and put on the web. Most of the traffic still comes from those guides, not the catalog.

2022

Second-generation transition begins.

Josh joins full-time after eight years at a research-orchard program in Oregon. She brings the rootstock-trial discipline and what she calls "ruthless catalog editing." Forty-eight varieties get cut. The catalog gets better.

2026

Eight thousand trees in the propagation bed.

An entirely new bed dedicated to disease-resistant apples. Six new pawpaw varieties under trial. The cold-storage capacity expanded to a third barn. We are not getting bigger; we are getting denser.

A note on regional propagation

“Why we don't ship a tree we didn't grow.”

A grafted tree is the product of two organisms — a scion that determines fruit, and a rootstock that determines vigor, hardiness, and disease tolerance. Both have to be matched to the climate they'll spend their life in.

Cold-climate propagation discards weak combinations the first winter. Mild-climate propagation does not. A nursery that grew its stock in the Pacific Northwest and shipped it East to be "finished" can't tell you which of its trees would have made it through a West Virginia February. We can.
