The Story of this slab.

This walnut was on the hillside long before Josh and Abigail bought the land in 2018. By count of growth rings — a hundred and eight at the butt — the tree was planted by no one. It seeded itself sometime around 1916, in the slot of an older oak that had blown down a few seasons before. The spot — bottom of the slope, near the spring — was a tree that wanted its feet near water finding them.

It grew straight, threw a good crown, and produced two bushels of nuts every other year for the better part of a century. The derecho that crossed Fayette County in June 2022 took out four trees on that slope. This was the largest. We brought a portable bandsaw onto the property the following Tuesday and milled the trunk into seventeen slabs.

The slab that goes into this table is the third one down from the butt. The four below it became four chairs. The eight above became a bench, two coffee tables, and the wood library you can see in the catalog. Three remain, drying.
