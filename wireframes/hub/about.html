What it is.

Gather at the Grove is a seventy-acre property in the Appalachian foothills near the New River Gorge and three independent businesses that operate on it. The property is held by a single family ownership. The businesses are separate, with separate books and separate legal structures, and they share the land, the journal, and a regenerative plan they jointly authored in 2022.

The hillside has a south-facing slope, a stream that runs year-round, a stand of black walnuts planted in First-gen, an old dairy barn that's now the workshop, a farmhouse from 1898 that's still the kitchen, and roughly four acres of bottomland that took us fifteen years to convince into being good soil.

The three businesses are: a heritage farm and preserve operation, a cold-climate fruit-tree nursery, and a one-bench furniture workshop. They overlap at the margins — the workshop mills timber from trees the nursery decided to take out, the farm uses preserves jars the workshop sometimes turns into spice racks — but mostly they run independently.

This site exists because the three of us realized in early 2024 that we'd been operating as one village without ever having published anything that said so.

The four pillars we keep coming back to.

Pillar № 01

Slow.

We measure work in seasons and stewardship in decades. The shops sell what's ready, when it's ready. Air-dried wood, mail-order timing, harvest windows — the calendar is the boss.

Pillar № 02

From here.

The trees came off the land. The compost feeds the rows it came from. The preserves go in jars from the next town over. Provenance is not a marketing word.

Pillar № 03

Honest about scale.

None of the three businesses is going to be bigger ten years from now in a way you can measure on a spreadsheet. They're going to be denser, deeper, more interesting to work in. That's the design choice.

Pillar № 04

The journal is the asset.

Seven years of field notes is what we actually have to give. The shops sell things; the journal is the longer answer.

Who we are.

Josh and Abigail Dunbar bought the original property in 2018. Rosa ran the farm side. Eli started the nursery in the same year, in what was then a 20'×40' rented hoophouse. Abigail, came home from an North Carolina research-orchard program in 2022 and is gradually taking over the nursery's catalog. George, Mae's brother, built his first piece of furniture from a fallen walnut in 2021 and ran the workshop full-time starting in 2022.

The four of us are not particularly evangelical about any of this. We're not trying to convince anyone to leave their job and start a farm. We are trying to do the work in front of us, in a particular place, with what's reasonable to ask of one family and four pairs of hands. The website is, in a real sense, the journal someone has to keep when there's no boss to write a report to.
