— Section 02 / Three shops, three trades —

The three shops of the grove.

Each runs as its own business with its own catalog, its own seasonal rhythm, and its own way of doing the work. The land — and the longer view — is what they share.

№ 01 — The Farm · Goldberrygrove.farm

Goldberry Grove —
the heritage farm.

The matriarch operation. Started in 2018 on twenty acres of hardpan. Today: twenty acres, one hundred-plus heritage hickory and apple trees, six rows of cut flowers and herbs, and a kitchen that runs the preserve operation from August through October.

The shop ships preserves, dried herbs, lavender wreaths and beeswax candles by mail. The farmstand at the foot of the drive runs on the honor system from June through November.

№ 02 — The Nursery · Atthegrovenursery.com

At The Grove —
the cold-climate nursery.

Cold-hardy fruit trees, berry stock, and edible perennials, propagated on-site since 2024. Three hundred and twenty-eight varieties in the current catalog, all grafted in our own propagation beds and lifted bare-root in February.

We ship bare-root to eight states from late February through April. The catalog is the work; the Field Guide is the explanation of why every choice in it was made the way it was.

Enter the catalog → Est. 2024 · Zone 3–7 · 82 varieties
№ 03 — The Workshop · Woodworkingeorge.com

GGG Woodworking —
the one-bench shop.

A small furniture workshop in the old dairy barn at the bottom of the property. Built one piece at a time from walnut, cherry, and white oak felled on site and air-dried four to six years before it ever reaches the bench.

Catalog pieces ship in twelve to fourteen weeks. Commissions run six to nine months. Twelve pieces a year, sometimes thirteen, never twenty. Provenance card on the underside of every piece — tree number, year planted, year felled, the storm.

— What the three shops share —

“Three businesses on the same hillside means three points of view on the same year.”

Shared · 01

The land.

Thirty-two acres, one ownership, one regenerative plan. The businesses are tenants of the soil, not the other way around.

Shared · 02

The journal.

One blog, three voices. Each business contributes its own field notes. The almanac is jointly authored.

Shared · 03

The kitchen table.

Monthly. The three operators sit down and argue about which crop, which trade-off, which year. The hub exists because of those conversations.

Shared · 04

The newsletter.

One email a month. The land, the journal, what's happening at each shop. No promo blasts.
